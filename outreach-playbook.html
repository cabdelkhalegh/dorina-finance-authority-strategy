Outreach & Networking Playbook

Building Dorina Salman’s Professional Network from a Clean Slate

“Money, made clear.” — “المال بوضوح.”

1. Networking Philosophy: Give First, Never Spam

You are starting from roughly 4 connections — and that is a genuine advantage. You get to build a network that is 100% intentional: every connection chosen because the relationship could be real. No dead weight, no random accumulation, no old contacts who knew a different version of you.

Five principles that govern everything in this playbook:

  1. Give before you ask. Comment on their work, share their post, answer their question, send them a useful template — before any request. People remember who showed up for them first.
  2. Personalize or don’t send. Every connection request gets a note referencing something specific about them. A blank request is a coin flip; a personal note is a conversation starter.
  3. Never pitch in the first message. Or the second. You are building relationships, not running a sales campaign. (There is nothing to sell yet anyway — which makes this period the perfect time to build goodwill with no agenda.)
  4. Quality beats volume. 10–15 thoughtful requests a day maximum. LinkedIn penalizes mass invitations, and so do humans.
  5. Every interaction is content research. The questions people ask you in DMs and comments become your next posts. The network feeds the engine.

2. Target Connection Map — Six Groups

Group A — Lebanese & Regional Finance Professionals and Accountants

Why: They are your future collaborators, referral partners, and credibility-by-association. Accountants constantly meet clients whose records are chaos — exactly the problem you solve at the organizational level. You are not their competitor; you are their upstream. How to find: LinkedIn search “accountant” / “auditor” / “financial controller” + Lebanon or Beirut; members of the Lebanese Association of Certified Public Accountants (LACPA) network; commenters on regional finance posts; Big Four alumni now at regional firms.

Group B — Small-Business Owners & Freelancers (Lebanon + GCC)

Why: Your core future audience and, eventually, Wudooh Finance’s client base. Their questions shape your content; their trust becomes your reputation. How to find: LinkedIn hashtags (#LebaneseBusiness, #FreelancersLebanon, #SmallBusinessMENA); founders posting about their shops, agencies, and studios; Lebanese entrepreneurship communities and pages; freelancer platforms’ community members who are active on LinkedIn.

Group C — Former Colleagues & AOU Alumni

Why: Your warm circle — people who already know your work ethic. They provide your first likes, first comments, and first endorsements, which teach the algorithm (and new visitors) that your profile is alive. How to find: LinkedIn’s “People you may know” once your profile lists Arab Open University and your work history; the AOU – Lebanon alumni page (“Alumni” tab on the university’s LinkedIn page lets you filter by year and field); colleagues from your law-firm years and current TiKiT circle.

Group D — HR Professionals & Recruiters at Regional Firms

Why: Your positioning is also career positioning. Recruiters searching for “financial operations,” “office manager finance,” or “administrative + finance” profiles in Lebanon and the GCC should find you. A visible, credible profile turns you from applicant into candidate-who-gets-approached. How to find: LinkedIn search “Talent Acquisition” / “Recruiter” + Lebanon, UAE, KSA, Qatar; recruiters at regional agencies who post job openings in finance/admin; HR managers at companies you’d genuinely consider.

Group E — Finance Content Creators (Arabic + English)

Why: Their audiences are your future audience. Consistent, intelligent commenting on their posts is the single fastest visibility engine available to a new account — and later, co-content with even one mid-size Arabic finance creator can outperform months of solo posting. How to find: Search LinkedIn for Arabic personal-finance and money-management content; follow hashtags like #PersonalFinance, #ماليات, #ادخار, #ريادة_أعمال; note who the algorithm surfaces once you engage; include regional financial-literacy platforms and their founders.

Group F — TiKiT Colleagues & Marketing-World Contacts

Why: Your current daily network — and secretly powerful: marketers understand content, distribution, and branding. They will amplify your early posts, give sharp feedback on your hooks, and many freelancers/agency owners in their orbit are Group B prospects. How to find: You already know them. Connect with everyone at TiKiT, then their engaged connections, plus agency owners and marketing freelancers in the Lebanese marketing scene.

3. The 4-Wave Connection Plan (Aligned to the 90-Day Plan)

Volume rule for all waves: 10–15 personalized requests per day maximum. Slower is fine; unpersonalized is not. Expect a 30–50% acceptance rate — that is normal and healthy.

Wave When Who Weekly target Purpose
Wave 1 — Warm Circle Days 1–14 (after profile rewrite is live) Groups C + F: colleagues, classmates, AOU alumni, TiKiT 30–50 requests/week Build the base audience before content ramps; guarantee early engagement
Wave 2 — Adjacent Professionals Days 15–35 Group A + first Group D: accountants, finance professionals, selected recruiters 50–70 requests/week Surround yourself with the profession you’re entering; learn its language
Wave 3 — Target Audience Days 36–65 Group B: small-business owners, freelancers 50–70 requests/week Grow the audience your content serves; harvest questions and topics
Wave 4 — Creators & Partners Days 66–90 Group E + deeper Group A: creators, potential collaborators 30–50 requests/week (plus daily commenting throughout) Open collaboration doors; begin partnership conversations

Standing rules across all waves: - Comment on a person’s post before sending the request whenever possible — acceptance rates roughly double. - After each wave, spend 2–3 days engaging with new connections’ content instead of sending more requests. - Log everything in the tracking sheet (Section 10). Memory is not a system.

4. Scripts — Paste-Ready Templates (EN + AR)

Adapt names and details; never send two identical messages to people who might know each other.

4.1 Connection Notes (4 variants by group)

Variant 1 — Finance professionals / accountants (Group A):

EN: Hi [Name], I’m a business administrator in Lebanon going deep into financial operations and bookkeeping, and I’m deliberately building a network of finance professionals I can learn from. Your post on [topic] was exactly the kind of practical thinking I appreciate. Would be glad to connect.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، أعمل في مجال إدارة الأعمال في لبنان وأتخصص حاليًا في العمليات المالية ومسك الدفاتر، وأحرص على بناء شبكة من المتخصصين الماليين أتعلم منهم. أعجبني منشورك حول [الموضوع] لطرحه العملي. يسعدني التواصل معك.

Variant 2 — Small-business owners / freelancers (Group B):

EN: Hi [Name], I loved seeing what you’re building with [business/project]. I share practical content on organizing business finances — cash flow, invoicing, keeping clean records — for small businesses here in Lebanon and the region. No pitch, just glad to connect with people doing real work.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، أعجبني ما تبنيه في [اسم المشروع]. أشارك محتوى عمليًا حول تنظيم الأمور المالية للأعمال الصغيرة — التدفق النقدي، الفواتير، والسجلات المنظمة — في لبنان والمنطقة. يسعدني التواصل مع أصحاب المشاريع الجادّين.

Variant 3 — Warm circle: colleagues & AOU alumni (Groups C/F):

EN: Hi [Name], it’s been a while! I’m rebuilding my LinkedIn presence around something I’ve become passionate about — financial clarity and money organization — and I wanted my network to start with people I actually know. Hope you’re doing well; would love to reconnect.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، مرّ وقت طويل! أعيد بناء حضوري على لينكد إن حول مجال أصبحت شغوفة به — الوضوح المالي وتنظيم الأموال — وأردت أن تبدأ شبكتي بأشخاص أعرفهم فعلًا. أتمنى أن تكون بخير، ويسعدني تجديد التواصل.

Variant 4 — Recruiters / HR (Group D):

EN: Hi [Name], I’m a business administrator in Lebanon (B.B.A., 12+ years of operations experience) currently specializing in financial operations and bookkeeping. I follow the roles you share in the region and would value being on your radar as I build in this direction. Glad to connect.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، أعمل في الإدارة في لبنان (إجازة في إدارة الأعمال وخبرة تفوق ١٢ عامًا في العمليات)، وأتخصص حاليًا في العمليات المالية ومسك الدفاتر. أتابع الفرص التي تشاركها في المنطقة ويسعدني أن أكون ضمن شبكتك المهنية.

4.2 Post-Accept Thank-You (send within 48 hours; expect nothing back)

EN: Thanks for connecting, [Name]! Quick intro: I help make money matters clearer — budgeting, cash flow, and financial organization for individuals and small businesses, in English and Arabic. If there’s ever a money-organization topic you’d like explained simply, tell me — reader questions become my best posts. No reply needed — glad to have you here.

AR: شكرًا لقبول التواصل، [الاسم]! تعريف سريع: أساعد في جعل الأمور المالية أوضح — الميزانية والتدفق النقدي والتنظيم المالي للأفراد والأعمال الصغيرة، بالعربية والإنجليزية. إن كان هناك موضوع مالي تودّ شرحه ببساطة، أخبرني — أسئلة القرّاء تتحول عندي إلى أفضل المنشورات. لا حاجة للرد — سعيدة بوجودك في شبكتي.

4.3 Value-First Follow-Up (2–4 weeks after connecting, only when genuinely relevant)

EN: Hi [Name], I remembered you mentioned [their challenge — e.g., chasing invoices / tracking cash] — I recently put together a simple [template/checklist] for exactly that, and I’d be happy to send it over. Free, no strings; if it’s useful, that’s the whole point.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، تذكرت أنك ذكرت [التحدي — مثل متابعة الفواتير / تتبع المصاريف النقدية] — أعددت مؤخرًا [قالبًا/قائمة تحقق] بسيطة لهذا الموضوع تحديدًا، ويسعدني إرسالها لك. مجانًا وبدون أي مقابل — إن كانت مفيدة فهذا هو الهدف.

4.4 Collaboration / Partnership Pitch (Month 3+, only after weeks of mutual engagement)

EN: Hi [Name], I’ve really valued exchanging ideas with you these past weeks. An idea: our audiences overlap but our angles differ — you cover [their strength, e.g., tax/accounting], I focus on financial organization and cash-flow clarity. Would you be open to a joint piece — a co-written post, or a short live Q&A — on [specific topic, e.g., “getting your records accountant-ready before year-end”]? I’d handle the drafting and organization; you bring the [expertise]. Happy to shape it however works for you.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، استفدت كثيرًا من تبادل الأفكار معك خلال الأسابيع الماضية. لديّ فكرة: جمهورانا متقاربان لكن زاويتانا مختلفتان — أنت تغطي [مجاله، مثل الضرائب/المحاسبة] وأنا أركّز على التنظيم المالي ووضوح التدفق النقدي. هل لديك استعداد لعمل مشترك — منشور نكتبه معًا أو لقاء قصير للأسئلة والأجوبة — حول [موضوع محدد، مثل “تجهيز سجلاتك قبل نهاية السنة”]؟ أتولى أنا الصياغة والتنظيم، وتضيف أنت خبرتك المتخصصة. ويسعدني ترتيبه بالطريقة التي تناسبك.

4.5 Informational Coffee-Chat Ask (for professionals you admire; ask for 20 minutes, not mentorship)

EN: Hi [Name], I’ve been following your work in [field] and learning a lot from it. I’m transitioning from business administration into financial operations, and I’d genuinely value 20 minutes of your perspective — how you built your path, and what you’d focus on first if you were starting today. Virtual or in person in [Beirut/area], whenever suits you. And if you’re too busy, no problem at all — I’ll keep learning from your posts.

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، أتابع عملك في [المجال] وأتعلم منه الكثير. أنتقل حاليًا من الإدارة إلى العمليات المالية، وسأقدّر كثيرًا ٢٠ دقيقة من وقتك — كيف بنيت مسارك، وما الذي كنت ستركّز عليه أولًا لو بدأت اليوم؟ عن بُعد أو حضوريًا في [بيروت/المنطقة]، في الوقت الذي يناسبك. وإن كان وقتك لا يسمح فلا مشكلة إطلاقًا — سأواصل التعلم من منشوراتك.

4.6 Recruiter Conversation Reply (when a recruiter reaches out)

EN: Hi [Name], thank you for reaching out — I appreciate it. Quick snapshot: 12+ years in business administration and operations, a B.B.A., and a current specialization in financial operations and bookkeeping (certificates in progress). I’m most interested in roles that combine administration with financial responsibility — operations, finance admin, office management with finance ownership. Happy to share my CV or schedule a call this week. Could you tell me more about the role and its finance scope?

AR: مرحبًا [الاسم]، شكرًا لتواصلك — أقدّر ذلك. نبذة سريعة: خبرة تفوق ١٢ عامًا في الإدارة والعمليات، إجازة في إدارة الأعمال، وتخصص حالي في العمليات المالية ومسك الدفاتر (شهادات قيد الإنجاز). أهتم بالأدوار التي تجمع بين الإدارة والمسؤولية المالية — العمليات، الإدارة المالية، أو إدارة المكاتب مع مهام مالية. يسعدني إرسال سيرتي الذاتية أو تحديد مكالمة هذا الأسبوع. هل يمكنك إخباري أكثر عن الدور ونطاقه المالي؟

5. Commenting Strategy — Your Fastest Visibility Engine

For a new account, comments outperform posts: a thoughtful comment on a large post puts you in front of thousands who would never otherwise see you. Target: 3–5 high-value comments daily (built into the 15-minute routine in CONTENT_AUTHORITY_ENGINE.md).

A high-value comment: - Is 2–5 sentences (one-liners are invisible; essays are skipped). - Adds one of: a concrete example, a complementary point, a respectful nuance, a sharp question, or a Lebanon/regional angle. - Never corrects rudely, never hijacks with self-promotion, never pastes a link.

Five example comments (adapt, don’t copy): 1. On an accountant’s post about messy client records: “This matches what I see from the operations side — the problem usually starts eleven months before tax season, in daily habits. A simple weekly 15-minute filing ritual would save clients money on your fees and save you the shoebox. Do you find clients accept small habit changes more easily than big system changes?” 2. On a budgeting post by a creator: “The ranges point deserves more attention. In Lebanon, where prices can shift between two grocery trips, exact-number budgets die in week one — ranges plus a ‘life happens’ line is the only design I’ve seen survive. Thanks for putting it so clearly.” 3. On a founder’s post about cash-flow stress: “The invisible part is often timing, not amount — money arriving on the 30th when obligations land on the 15th. A one-page month-ahead cash calendar makes that visible fast. Congratulations on how you handled this quarter.” 4. On a recruiter’s post about standout candidates: “Would add: candidates who can show a system they built — a tracker, a process, a checklist — stand out even more than ones who list responsibilities. Systems are proof, titles are claims.” 5. On an Arabic post about saving: “نقطة مهمة جدًا. أضيف من تجربتي: الادخار الذي ينجح هو الذي يحدث تلقائيًا في بداية الشهر، لا ما يتبقى في آخره — لأن ‘ما يتبقى’ نادرًا ما يتبقى. شكرًا على الطرح الواضح.”

6. DM Etiquette & Red Flags

Etiquette: - Never pitch anything in a first message — no services, no links, no “quick call?” - Match their language (Arabic to Arabic, English to English) and their formality level. - Reply to every genuine DM within 24–48 hours; a short warm reply beats a delayed perfect one. - If someone sends you spam, decline politely once, then disengage. Never argue in DMs.

Hard red lines (see RISK_ETHICS_AND_COMPLIANCE.md for the full framework): - Never give personalized investment, tax, or legal advice in DMs — no “should I buy dollars/gold/crypto,” no “which bank,” no tax questions. Standard reply: “That’s a decision that needs a licensed professional who knows your full situation — I don’t give personalized investment/tax advice. What I can help with is organizing the numbers so that conversation is productive.” - Never discuss anyone’s specific finances in public comments — move sensitive threads to DM, and even there, stay organizational. - Never share screenshots of private conversations, even anonymized, without explicit permission. - Decline any “partnership” involving promoting financial products, trading platforms, or investment schemes — one such post can destroy two years of credibility.

7. Partnership Plays (Month 3 Onward)

  1. Co-posts with accountants (Group A). You write the plain-language organizational half; they add the technical accounting half. Both audiences see both names. Start with the accountant you’ve engaged with most.
  2. Mini-webinar / LinkedIn Live with an accountant or auditor. 30–45 minutes: “Getting Your Records Accountant-Ready.” You moderate and cover organization; they answer technical questions. Recording becomes a Featured-section asset.
  3. Community group value drops. Join Lebanese entrepreneur and freelancer groups (LinkedIn, Facebook, WhatsApp). Contribute answers for weeks before ever mentioning your content; when someone asks a money-organization question, answer fully in-thread — the profile visits follow on their own.
  4. AOU alumni events. Attend alumni gatherings and offer the alumni office a free 45-minute session — “Personal Money Clarity for New Graduates.” Universities constantly need practical speakers; alumni speaker credit is credibility you can post about.
  5. Creator exchanges (Group E). Offer Arabic adaptations of an English creator’s best framework (with full credit and their agreement) or a “5 questions with…” interview post. Interview formats let you borrow authority gracefully while giving genuine value.
  6. Template collaborations. Build a bilingual template with a partner (e.g., an invoice tracker reviewed by an accountant: “reviewed by [Name], CPA”). Their review costs them 20 minutes and gives your asset a stamp of professional confidence.

8. Offline Networking in Lebanon

Online authority converts fastest when reinforced in person — and Lebanon’s business world still runs substantially on personal relationships.

  • Entrepreneurship & startup meetups: Beirut’s ecosystem events, incubator open days, and startup community gatherings (follow Beirut Digital District and similar hubs’ event calendars). Go with a one-line intro ready: “I help individuals and small businesses organize their money and understand their cash flow.”
  • Chambers of commerce: The Chamber of Commerce, Industry and Agriculture of Beirut & Mount Lebanon (CCIA-BML) and regional chambers run SME workshops and networking events — exactly your Group B in physical form.
  • University events: AOU alumni events first (warmest door), then guest-session offers to business faculties once you have 6+ months of content to show.
  • Professional association events: LACPA and finance-community seminars welcome attendees; going as a learner is both genuine and a Group A networking goldmine.
  • TiKiT’s own network: marketing agencies host and attend client events constantly — quietly one of your best offline channels.

After every offline conversation: connect on LinkedIn within 24 hours with a note referencing the conversation. Offline → online capture is the whole point.

10. Tracking Sheet Structure

One simple spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel), one row per person. Update in the Friday five minutes — do not let it become a project.

Column Content
Name Full name
Group A–F (from the target map)
Wave 1–4
Date requested When you sent the request
Accepted? Y / N / Pending
Thank-you sent Date
Last interaction Date + one word (comment / DM / call / event)
Notes What they care about, challenges they mentioned, content ideas they sparked
Next step One concrete action + rough date (e.g., “send invoice template — early Sep”)
Warmth Cold / Warm / Hot (Hot = replies, engages, potential partner)

Monthly habit: sort by Warmth, pick the top 5 “Hot” relationships, and invest deliberately in them next month. Five real relationships per month = 60 meaningful professional allies in year one — from a starting point of four connections. That is how a clean slate becomes a network.
