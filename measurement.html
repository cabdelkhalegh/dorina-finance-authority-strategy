Measurement Dashboard — KPIs & Decision Checkpoints

For: Dorina Salman Purpose: One simple measurement system for the whole journey: a 5-minute weekly log, a 30-minute monthly review, and evidence-based decision gates at Day 90, Month 6, Month 12, and Month 24. Time cost: ~5 min/week + ~30 min/month. That’s all. Measurement should serve the work, never compete with it.

The principle behind this dashboard: feelings are terrible navigators — they say “quit” on a slow week and “expand” on a lucky one. Numbers, reviewed calmly once a month, are how this plan makes decisions. Operating Principle 10 in MASTER_STRATEGIC_PLAN.md lives here: review monthly, decide at checkpoints.

1. What You Measure — and Why It’s Not “Likes”

Two kinds of numbers matter, and they play different roles:

Type What it tells you Examples
Leading indicators (effort you control) Whether you’re doing the work that causes growth Posts published, connection requests sent, engagement minutes done, certificate hours studied
Lagging indicators (results that follow) Whether the work is landing Connections, followers, DM conversations, template requests, inbound questions

The rule: you are accountable for leading indicators and patient with lagging ones. A month where you published 12 posts and sent 100 personalized requests is a successful month even if follower growth was modest — the results catch up. The reverse (great numbers, no system) is luck, and luck doesn’t compound.

Likes are deliberately absent from your KPI tables. A like is a passing tap; it builds nothing. The signals that predict real authority, in ascending order of value: likes < comments < saves/shares < DM conversations < inbound requests for help. Track the right end of that chain.

2. The Weekly 5-Minute Log (Every Sunday)

Keep one simple spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel — you’ll build far fancier ones for your audience). One row per week, filled in every Sunday evening as part of your planning anchor. This is the sheet 90_DAY_ACTION_PLAN.md refers to — the monthly and checkpoint reviews read straight off it.

Columns:

Column What to enter
Week # / date e.g., “W7 — Mar 2”
Connections (total) Current total, from your profile
Followers (total) Current total
Posts published this week Count, e.g., “3”
…of which Arabic/bilingual Count, e.g., “1”
Comments received on my posts Count across the week’s posts
Replied within 24 hrs? Yes / mostly / no
Meaningful DM conversations Count (definition below)
Template / kit requests Count this week
Certificate progress % or module, e.g., “Module 3 of 8”
Engagement days done Out of the days you planned, e.g., “5/6”
Best post this week One line: topic + why you think it worked
Inbound signals Anything unprompted: a question, a referral, “can you help me with…” — log every single one

Definitions that keep the numbers honest:

  • Meaningful DM conversation = a two-way exchange about someone’s actual money-organization situation or your content — not a connection auto-thank-you. (Same definition as in 90_DAY_ACTION_PLAN.md — use it consistently.)
  • Inbound signal = anything you did not initiate: a question in comments or DMs, a tag, a referral, a “do you do this for clients?” These are the most precious rows in the sheet — from Month 12 onward they drive the consulting decision, so log them from day one with a date and one line of context.
  • Counting rule: enter real numbers only. No rounding up, no “roughly.” A dashboard you flatter is a dashboard you can’t steer by — and Operating Principle 8 (small numbers, honestly grown) applies to your own tracking first.

3. The Monthly Review — 30 Minutes, Same Day Each Month

Last Sunday of each month, 30 minutes, coffee optional but recommended. Work through five steps and write the answers into a “Monthly” tab — one short block per month.

Step 1 — Collect (5 min). Totals for the month from the weekly rows: posts (EN/AR split), new connections, new followers, meaningful DMs, template requests, certificate progress, inbound signals.

Step 2 — Compare (5 min). Against the target table for wherever you are in the plan (Section 4 for the first 90 days; Section 6 afterwards). Mark each metric ✅ on-track / ⚠️ behind / ❌ well behind.

Step 3 — Learn (10 min). Answer in one or two sentences each: 1. Which post got the most comments or saves this month — and what was its topic, format, and language? 2. Which post underperformed — and what’s your best guess why? 3. What did people ask about (comments, DMs) that you haven’t written about yet? → add to your content-ideas list. 4. Which language drove more conversations this month — Arabic or English? (This steers the ratio per CONTENT_AUTHORITY_ENGINE.md.)

Step 4 — Decide ONE change (5 min). Exactly one adjustment for next month — repeat the best-performing format, shift the language mix, change connection targets, adjust posting time. One, not five: change one variable and you’ll know next month whether it worked.

Step 5 — Note the wins (5 min). Write down 2–3 things that genuinely went well, however small. You will reread these on discouraged days, and they matter more than you think right now.

Monthly review template (copy into your sheet):

MONTH: ___
Numbers: posts __ (AR __) | new connections __ | new followers __ |
meaningful DMs __ | template requests __ | certificate __% | inbound signals __
Status vs. targets: ___
Best post & why: ___
Weakest post & why: ___
Audience asked about: ___
The ONE change for next month: ___
Wins: 1) ___  2) ___  3) ___

4. Checkpoint 1 — The Day-90 Review

Run this at the end of Week 13, per 90_DAY_ACTION_PLAN.md. Score yourself against the same table you’ve been working toward all quarter:

Metric Day-0 Baseline Day-90 Target Minimum Acceptable Your Number
Connections ~4 400–500+ 250
Followers 4 300+ 150
Posts published 0 35+ 25
Arabic / bilingual posts 0 8+ 5
Certificate #1 none completed or >60% enrolled + >30%
Meaningful DM conversations 0 5+/week (weeks 10–13) 3+/week
Lead magnet none published, 30+ requests published
Comment reply rate (24 hrs) — 100% 90%
Free clarity chats held 0 2–4 1

Decision rules:

  • Most metrics at target → Horizon 2 begins. Move to the Section 6 dashboard, keep the same weekly log, and publish your checkpoint post — real numbers, real lessons. Transparency about the climb is authority content.
  • Most metrics between minimum and target, trending up → also a pass. The minimum-acceptable column with a clear upward trend advances the plan; perfection was never the gate.
  • Metrics below minimum → that’s a signal, not a failure. Do not start Horizon 2 on a weak foundation. Run the diagnosis below, fix the single biggest issue, and repeat a corrected Weeks 8–13 block first. The plan’s timeline bends; its sequence doesn’t.

The Diagnostic Clinic — When Numbers Fall Short

Find your symptom, check the likely causes in order (the first three account for most cases), apply the fix for 4 weeks, then re-measure. Fix one thing at a time.

Symptom Most likely cause The fix
Connections low, acceptance rate poor Requests not personalized, or aimed at random profiles instead of the target segments Every request gets one personal line (scripts in OUTREACH_AND_NETWORKING_PLAYBOOK.md); target only the four audience segments from MASTER_STRATEGIC_PLAN.md §3.3
Posts published but little engagement Posts too general — big-topic essays instead of one concrete problem per post Return to the pillar formats: one idea, one example (a shop, a salary, a receipt drawer), one takeaway. Repeat the format of your two best posts with new topics
Everything flat, growth stalled Engagement minutes skipped — publishing without participating The 15 min/day of thoughtful comments is not optional garnish; it is how new audiences find you. Restore it before changing anything else
Followers grow, but zero DMs or requests Content informs but never invites Add the CTA lines from BILINGUAL_COPY_BANK.md; ask genuine questions; offer the template explicitly
Arabic posts underperform English (or vice versa) Mismatch between topic and language audience Follow the language-pairing guidance in BILINGUAL_COPY_BANK.md §9 — everyday-money topics in Arabic, career/professional topics in English
Comment replies slipping past 24 hrs Rhythm overload Batch replies twice daily (morning, evening), 5 minutes each. If still slipping, drop to 2 posts/week for a month rather than dropping replies — conversation beats broadcast
You’re exhausted Doing more than the plan asks Cut back to the 5–7 hr/week budget or to minimum viable mode (90_DAY_ACTION_PLAN.md, “What If I Fall Behind?”). Consistency beats intensity — that’s a rule, not a slogan

5. Checkpoint 2 — Month 6: The Newsletter Decision

CONTENT_AUTHORITY_ENGINE.md holds the newsletter until at least Month 6 and points here for the go/no-go. Start it only if all four are true:

  • The 3-posts/week rhythm has held for 8+ of the last 10 weeks (life-interruptions allowed).
  • 500+ connections and a lead magnet with 50+ total requests — proof there’s an audience to invite.
  • You genuinely have one spare hour a week — the newsletter is 80% recycled from your strongest posts, but assembly still takes time.
  • You want to. A newsletter abandoned after three issues costs credibility; one started at Month 8 instead of 6 costs nothing.

If any box is unchecked: not yet. Re-check monthly. There is no penalty for a later start — only for a broken promise to subscribers.

Month-12 gate (Horizon 2 exit). Substantially met → you are a recognized niche voice; begin the pre-consulting pilot phase described in FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §8, and start the quarterly launch-criteria reviews below. Behind but climbing → simply continue; Horizon 2 can take 15–18 months without any harm to the plan. The gates measure readiness, not speed.

7. The Horizon-3 Tracker (Months 12–24) — Feeding the Consulting Decision

From Month 12, add two small tabs to your sheet. They exist to make the Month-24 decision for you, with evidence instead of mood.

Tab: Inbound demand log. Date · who (role/segment) · what they asked for · would they have paid? (yes / maybe / no) · outcome. The launch gate in FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md requires 5+ genuine inbound requests per month for paid help, sustained 3 consecutive months — this log is the only honest way to know when that’s true.

Tab: Pilot log. For each pilot engagement (per blueprint §8): client type · package · fee · hours actually spent · outcome in one line · testimonial (consented? Y/N) · what you’d change. Pilots are rehearsals; the log is where the learning accumulates — and where your real effective hourly rate emerges, which later sets honest pricing.

Quarterly (Months 12, 15, 18, 21, 24): score the full Launch Criteria checklist in FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §9 — content consistency, certificate portfolio, demand signal, proof of delivery, 6-month runway, professional bench, gut check. Write the date and the count: “Month 15: 3 of 7.” Watching the number climb quarter by quarter is the calmest possible way to approach a life decision.

8. Checkpoint 3 — The Month-24 Decision

Three outcomes, all legitimate:

  • GO — all 7 launch criteria checked. Begin the incorporation path in FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §7. You’re not gambling; you’re formalizing something that demonstrably already works.
  • NOT YET — 5–6 of 7 checked. Name the missing criteria, set a 2-quarter plan to close them, re-score at Month 30. Most good launches pass through “not yet” — it’s the gate doing its job.
  • NO / DIFFERENT PATH — the demand is real but you’ve discovered you prefer employment, or a senior financial-operations role has emerged, or life points elsewhere. This is a win, not a failure. By Month 24 you hold certificates, an audience, a portfolio, and career options that did not exist at Day 0. As MASTER_STRATEGIC_PLAN.md says: the plan wins on every branch.

Whichever branch you take, write one page: the numbers, the reasons, the decision. Future-you will want the record.

9. Measurement Rules — Read Once a Quarter

  1. Never skip the weekly log. Five minutes. The whole system rests on this habit; everything else reads off it.
  2. Never decide on a bad day. Decisions happen at monthly reviews and checkpoints — with the sheet open — not at 11 p.m. after a post flopped.
  3. Compare only to your own last month. Someone else’s follower count is not data about you. Your trend line is the only chart that matters.
  4. A red month is information, not indictment. The Diagnostic Clinic exists precisely because some months will miss. Diagnose, fix one thing, continue.
  5. Log every inbound signal, from day one. They’ll be rare at first, then a trickle, then a pattern. The pattern is your future business telling you it exists.
  6. Real numbers only. No bought followers, no engagement pods, no inflated counts — in public or in your own sheet. The dashboard only steers if it’s true.
  7. Celebrate on schedule. Step 5 of the monthly review is mandatory. Sustainable ambition runs on acknowledged progress.

What gets measured calmly, monthly, and honestly — gets built.
