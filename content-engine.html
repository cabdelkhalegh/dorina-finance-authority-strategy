Content Authority Engine

The 12-Month Publishing System for Dorina Salman

“Money, made clear.” — “المال بوضوح.”

1. Content Philosophy: Teach What You’re Learning

Most people wait until they feel like an expert before they publish. That is exactly backwards — and it is why most people never publish at all.

Your content strategy is built on one honest, powerful idea: document the journey from business administrator to finance-clarity specialist, in public, as it happens.

This works for three reasons:

  1. Honesty is a strategy, not a compromise. You are not pretending to be a CFO or a licensed advisor — and you never will in this plan. You are a business administrator with a B.B.A. and 12 years of real operational experience who is systematically mastering financial organization, budgeting, cash-flow clarity, and bookkeeping literacy. Saying that out loud makes you more credible, not less, because audiences are exhausted by fake gurus.
  2. Learners teach better than experts. You still remember what it feels like not to understand a cash-flow statement. The expert forgot years ago. Your explanations will land where theirs go over people’s heads.
  3. A clean slate is an advantage. With almost no existing LinkedIn activity, there is no old content to contradict your new positioning. You get to build the record from post one — deliberately, consistently, in your own voice.

Your permanent editorial rules: - Teach only what you have actually done, learned, or verified. If you learned it yesterday, say so — that is the story. - Never give personalized investment, tax, or legal advice. Your lane is organization, budgeting, cash-flow clarity, bookkeeping literacy, and small-business financial operations. (See RISK_ETHICS_AND_COMPLIANCE.md.) - Every post should leave the reader with one thing they can do today. - Warm, direct, practical. No jargon without a plain-language translation. No hype, no shame, no “you’re bad with money” framing — ever.

2. The Five Content Pillars in Depth

Pillar 1 — Money Basics Made Simple

Purpose: Build trust with individuals by making foundational money concepts feel obvious and doable. This is your widest-reach pillar and your kindest one.

Example topics (adapt freely): 1. The 3-account system: one for bills, one for life, one for goals 2. What “cash flow” actually means — explained with a kitchen tap and a drain 3. Why budgets fail (hint: it’s the budget’s fault, not yours) 4. Fixed vs. variable expenses — and why the split changes everything 5. The 24-hour rule before any non-essential purchase 6. How to read your own month: a 20-minute end-of-month money review 7. Emergency fund basics: how much, where, and what actually counts as an emergency 8. “Where did my salary go?” — a simple expense-tracking method that survives real life

Format mix: Short text posts, 5–7 slide carousels, single-image explainers, occasional 1-question polls. EN/AR guidance: Both languages, and this pillar is where Arabic matters most — publish the Arabic version as a full standalone post, not a translation footnote. Household money topics reach furthest in Arabic.

Pillar 2 — Small-Business Money Ops

Purpose: Position you for the professional audience — freelancers, shop owners, and small-business founders in Lebanon and the region who are brilliant at their craft and drowning in financial admin. This pillar feeds directly into the future consulting option.

Example topics: 1. The one spreadsheet every freelancer needs before they need an accountant 2. Separating personal and business money — the first real act of running a business 3. Invoicing hygiene: numbering, follow-up cadence, and getting paid on time 4. A simple weekly cash-flow check-in for a small business (15 minutes, one page) 5. What your accountant wishes you brought them (and in what format) 6. Pricing sanity check: do you actually know your monthly break-even? 7. Petty cash without chaos: a system that works in a cash economy 8. The end-of-month closing checklist for a one-person business

Format mix: Checklists, carousels, template screenshots, short “here’s how I’d organize this” walkthroughs. EN/AR guidance: Lead in English for GCC/professional reach; publish Arabic versions of the most practical pieces (invoicing, cash handling, separating accounts) for the local audience.

Pillar 3 — The Learning Journey

Purpose: Turn your upskilling into content. Every certificate module, every concept that finally clicked, every mistake — it all becomes proof that your expertise is real and earned in public. This is the pillar that makes your credibility ladder visible: Organizer → Educator → Certified practitioner → Consultant.

Example topics: 1. Why I’m going deep on finance after 12 years in business administration (your origin story) 2. Week 1 of my bookkeeping certificate: three things that surprised me 3. Debits and credits finally clicked — here’s the explanation I wish I’d had 4. What a B.B.A. teaches you about money — and what it doesn’t 5. I rebuilt my own budget using what I learned this month. Results inside. 6. Certificate milestone post: what this course covered, what I can now do 7. The hardest concept so far — and how I broke it down 8. Six months in: what’s changed in how I see money

Format mix: Story posts (text-first), milestone posts with the certificate image, “what I learned this week” recaps. EN/AR guidance: Both. Story posts translate beautifully into Arabic and feel personal; milestone posts work in English with an Arabic closing line.

Pillar 4 — Money in Lebanon & the Region

Purpose: This is your differentiation moat. Generic Western finance advice ignores multi-currency wallets, cash-heavy daily life, and banking realities in Lebanon. You live this context. Cover it with practical, calm, service-minded content — never political commentary, no blame, no doom. Just: “here is how to stay organized inside this reality.”

Example topics: 1. Budgeting when your income and expenses are in two (or three) currencies 2. A simple system for tracking cash spending when most of life is in cash 3. “Fresh” vs. bank money: how to label and track them without confusion in one ledger 4. How small Lebanese businesses can keep clean records in a dual-currency environment 5. Sending and receiving remittances: an organization checklist (not advice on providers) 6. Pricing your freelance services in USD, LBP, or both — a decision framework 7. Keeping receipts and records in an economy that runs on paper and WhatsApp 8. A family money meeting agenda for households juggling multiple currencies

Format mix: Practical frameworks, checklists, relatable scenario posts (“Sara earns in USD and pays rent in LBP…” — clearly framed as illustrative examples, never as real client stories). EN/AR guidance: Arabic-first. This pillar exists for the regional audience; English versions second, for the diaspora and GCC professionals.

Pillar 5 — Tools & Templates

Purpose: Give people something they can hold. Templates are the fastest trust-builder there is: they prove competence, they get saved and shared, and they later become your lead magnet and workshop material.

Example topics: 1. My monthly personal budget template (free, walkthrough included) 2. A one-page cash-flow tracker for freelancers 3. The end-of-month review checklist (printable) 4. A simple invoice template with a follow-up schedule built in 5. Dual-currency expense log — built for Lebanon 6. Debt-clarity worksheet: every obligation on one page (organizational, not advisory) 7. The “money folder” structure: how to organize financial documents digitally 8. Small-business record-keeping starter kit: five sheets, zero accounting degree required

Format mix: Screenshot + “comment/DM and I’ll send it” posts, short walkthrough carousels, before/after examples using illustrative data. EN/AR guidance: Build every template bilingual (EN/AR column headers or two tabs) — this alone will set you apart. Announce in both languages.

POST 2 — [P1] “Your budget didn’t fail. Your budget was designed to fail.” — fully drafted (EN; publish AR adaptation the following week)

Your budget didn’t fail. Your budget was designed to fail.

Here’s what most first budgets look like: a perfect spreadsheet built on a motivated Sunday, with categories like “Groceries: exactly $250” and zero room for real life.

Then Tuesday happens. A pharmacy run. A family lunch you didn’t plan. A price that changed since last month — which, in Lebanon, is not exactly rare.

By Friday the numbers are wrong, and by the next week the spreadsheet is abandoned. Sound familiar?

The problem was never your discipline. It was the design. A budget that breaks the first time life happens is a bad budget.

Three design fixes that actually work:

1️⃣ Budget in ranges, not exact numbers. “Groceries: $220–280” survives reality. “$250.00” doesn’t.

2️⃣ Add a “life happens” category — on purpose. Give the unplanned a planned home (5–10% of spending). Now surprises have a budget line instead of breaking the budget.

3️⃣ Review weekly for 10 minutes, don’t track daily. Daily tracking burns people out in two weeks. A short Sunday check-in is sustainable for years.

A budget is not a test you pass or fail. It’s a map you update.

Which of the three fixes do you need most? Tell me below — and if you’d like the simple template I use, say “template” in the comments.

CTA: Comment which fix you need / say “template”. Language: EN now, AR adaptation as a separate post in week 2.

6. Weekly Rhythm & Batching Workflow

The rhythm (non-negotiable for 90 days):

Day Slot Pillar rotation
Monday Educational post P1 / P2 / P4 rotating
Wednesday Tool / template / checklist P5 (or a practical P2/P4 piece)
Friday Story / journey / question P3 (or Skeleton D/E from any pillar)
Daily 15 min engagement See Section 8

The Sunday 90-minute batching session:

  • 0:00–0:10 — Review. Skim last week’s posts: what got comments? What got template requests? Note one lesson.
  • 0:10–0:25 — Choose. Pick the week’s 3 topics from this document (Section 4 first, then pillar topic lists). Match each to a skeleton.
  • 0:25–1:05 — Draft. Write all three posts back to back (roughly 13 minutes each — the skeletons make this possible). Don’t polish while drafting.
  • 1:05–1:20 — Polish & translate. Edit for warmth and brevity; prepare the Arabic version for whichever post is bilingual this week.
  • 1:20–1:30 — Stage. Schedule or save all three as drafts with their dates. Prepare any image/screenshot.

Result: the week’s publishing takes zero creative energy on weekdays — you only press post and engage. If a week collapses, the rule is: post fewer, never post zero. One post in a bad week keeps the streak alive; a silent week is the only real failure mode.

7. Arabic / English Strategy

Default logic: - Both languages (as two separate posts, 2–3 days apart — never one mixed wall of text): identity posts, money-basics posts, templates, milestones. Separate posts mean each language gets its own reach and its own comment section. - English-lead: Pillar 2 small-business ops, career/professional topics, collaboration posts with English-speaking professionals, anything aimed at GCC recruiters and firms. - Arabic-first: all of Pillar 4 (Lebanon & region), household/family money topics, cash-economy content. This is where Arabic-language finance content is genuinely scarce — your biggest open field.

Quality bar for Arabic: polished, professional Modern Standard Arabic that sounds natural when read aloud — never machine-literal translation. Adapt idioms and examples; a good Arabic post is a rewrite, not a translation. Keep numerals consistent within a post.

Practical ratio: aim over any month for roughly 60% of posts to exist in Arabic (original or adapted). Track which language drives more conversations in your monthly review (MEASUREMENT_DASHBOARD.md) and shift weight accordingly.

8. Engagement Rules — The Daily 15 Minutes

Posting without engaging is shouting into a room and leaving. The 15 minutes matter more than the posts in the first 90 days.

The routine (set a timer): - Min 0–5: Reply to every comment and DM on your own posts. A reply with a follow-up question doubles the thread. - Min 5–12: Leave 3–5 substantive comments on posts by people in your target map (see OUTREACH_AND_NETWORKING_PLAYBOOK.md): finance professionals, small-business owners, regional creators. Minimum two sentences; add an insight, an example, or a good question — never “Great post! 👏”. - Min 12–15: Accept/send connection requests per the outreach playbook; note anything interesting in your tracking sheet.

Rules: 1. Comment on others before expecting comments on yours. Give first. 2. Never argue about politics, religion, or the economy’s causes. Your lane is practical clarity. Disengage politely from bait. 3. Never give personalized financial advice in comments or DMs. Standard redirect: “That depends on your specific situation — for decisions like that, a licensed advisor/accountant is the right person. What I can share is how to organize the information so that conversation goes well.” 4. If a comment teaches you something, say so publicly. Learners who credit others build trust fastest.

9. Repurposing — One Post, Many Lives

Phase 1 (Months 1–6): LinkedIn only. One platform done consistently beats three done sporadically. Do not split focus.

Phase 2 (Month 6+, only if the rhythm is effortless): - WhatsApp: turn your best checklists into shareable images (branded: Deep Teal #0F4C5C, Warm Gold #D4A937, Ivory #FAF7F0; Tajawal for Arabic). WhatsApp is Lebanon’s real distribution network — one shared checklist in family and business groups can outperform a month of LinkedIn reach. Later, a WhatsApp broadcast channel can carry the monthly digest. - Instagram: carousels port almost one-to-one from LinkedIn; Arabic-first content performs especially well there. Open this door only when a content backlog exists — launch with 15 ready pieces, never from zero.

Evergreen recycling rule: after 90 days, any strong post may be reposted with a fresh hook and updated example. Your audience in Month 8 never saw Month 1. Compilation posts (like Post 29) are the cheapest high-value content you’ll ever make.

The compounding endpoint: every template → future lead magnet. Every series → future workshop outline. Every answered question → future FAQ for Wudooh Finance, when that day comes. Nothing you publish is disposable; you are building inventory for the consulting company while building the audience that will hire it.

This engine only works plugged into the rest of the package: profile foundation in LINKEDIN_PROFILE_REWRITE.md, distribution in OUTREACH_AND_NETWORKING_PLAYBOOK.md, scorekeeping in MEASUREMENT_DASHBOARD.md, and boundaries in RISK_ETHICS_AND_COMPLIANCE.md.
