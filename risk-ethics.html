Risk, Ethics & Compliance — The Rules That Protect You

For: Dorina Salman Status: The one document in this package that is not adjustable. Everything else — headlines, post drafts, timing — is yours to reshape. These rules are fixed, because your entire brand is built on trust, and trust has no minor breaches. When to read: fully, once, in Week 1 — before your first post. Then skim the referral-triggers table (Section 5) monthly until the reflexes are automatic.

The paradox that makes this document your friend: boundaries are not a brake on your authority — they are the engine of it. In a space crowded with overclaiming, the person who says clearly “that’s outside my scope, and here’s who to ask” becomes the one people trust, tag, and refer. Every rule below makes you more credible, not less.

1. Your Lane — In Scope vs. Out of Scope

Everything you publish, teach, and (eventually) sell lives inside the left column. Everything in the right column is referred out — warmly, every time, no exceptions.

✅ IN SCOPE — yours ❌ OUT OF SCOPE — refer out
Financial organization: systems, routines, record-keeping Investment advice: what to buy, hold, or sell — stocks, funds, crypto, gold, property-as-investment, any asset
Budgeting: frameworks, templates, category design, multi-currency practicality Tax advice: what to declare, deduct, structure, or file
Cash-flow clarity: tracking, forecasting basics, the 13-week view Legal advice: contracts, company structures, disputes, employment terms
Bookkeeping literacy: what the records mean, how to keep them clean Predictions: exchange rates, inflation, markets, “what the economy will do”
Small-business money ops: invoicing, separating personal/business money, owner dashboards Debt restructuring / negotiation with banks or creditors on someone’s behalf
Financial education: explaining statements, concepts, and named frameworks Insurance product selection: which policy or provider to choose
Referring people to the right licensed professional Religious rulings on money (e.g., what is halal/haram in finance): refer to qualified scholars; you may neutrally explain that such questions exist and deserve qualified answers
Handling anyone’s actual money or accounts — a Rung-4 activity requiring a proper written engagement, never before, never informally

The one-sentence test before anything you write or say: “Am I explaining how money can be organized and understood — or am I telling a specific person what to do with theirs?” The first is your work. The second is not — not in posts, not in comments, not in DMs, not verbally in a clarity chat, not “just this once” for a friend.

Educational comparisons stay educational. You may teach snowball vs. avalanche debt methods, 50/30/20 vs. zero-based budgeting, and similar frameworks — as comparisons with trade-offs, letting the reader choose. The line is crossed the moment you tell an individual which one they should apply to their situation with their real numbers. Teach the map; don’t drive their car.

2. Titles & Claims — Exact Phrasing Rules by Ladder Rung

Public claims may reference only artifacts that already exist (the ladder rule from MASTER_STRATEGIC_PLAN.md §4). Here is precisely what that means in words:

Never, at any rung, in any language

Do not call yourself — or let a bio, event flyer, podcast intro, or client describe you as — any of the following, and correct it politely when others do:

  • CFO · accountant · CPA · auditor
  • Licensed financial advisor · wealth manager · investment advisor
  • Tax advisor · tax consultant · lawyer
  • “Expert” in anything you hold no credential or substantial track record in

If a workshop host introduces you as “financial expert Dorina Salman,” open with a friendly correction: “Almost — I’m a finance-clarity specialist, and part of my job is being precise about titles, starting with my own.” That sentence wins the room.

Rung 1 — Organizer (now)

You may say: “Business administrator with a B.B.A. and twelve years of operations experience” · “finance-clarity specialist” (a descriptive positioning, not a protected title — always paired with your real background) · “I help people organize their money and understand their cash flow” · “sharing my own systems and what I’m learning.” Not yet: “bookkeeper,” “certified” anything, “consultant,” “coach” as a professional title.

Rung 2 — Educator (content engine running)

Add: “financial educator / I create educational content on money organization” · “I build budgeting and cash-flow templates.” Still not: any certification-based wording — until the certificate is completed, not merely enrolled. “Currently studying for [exact course name]” is honest and, per the learning-in-public strategy, actively good content.

Rung 3 — Certified Practitioner (certificate completed)

Add, using the issuer’s exact wording: “Holder of the [exact certificate name, issuer, year]” — e.g., Intuit Academy Bookkeeping Professional Certificate → you may then say “certified in bookkeeping” or “certified bookkeeper” where that matches the issuer’s language · “QuickBooks Online ProAdvisor” / “Xero Advisor Certified” once each exam is actually passed. Never inflate a certificate into a profession: a bookkeeping certificate does not make you an accountant; a coaching certificate does not make you an advisor. List credentials verbatim in LinkedIn’s Licenses & Certifications section, with issuer and date, the day you earn them — and never a day before.

Rung 4 — Consultant (only after the launch gate)

Add: “Founder of Wudooh Finance — a financial-clarity and financial-operations practice” · “consultant in financial organization and small-business money operations.” Forever excluded, even at Rung 4: everything in the “Never” list. The firm’s engagement letters must state its educational/organizational scope explicitly (see FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §7).

Profile audit rule: every time you update your headline, About, or bio — in either language — reread it against this section. Ninety seconds, every time. Accidental overclaim is still overclaim.

3. Disclaimers — Paste-Ready, Non-Negotiable

3.1 Standard line — every educational post, template, and guide

(Identical to BILINGUAL_COPY_BANK.md §8 — always use it verbatim.)

  • EN: Educational content only — not investment, tax, or legal advice.
  • AR: محتوى تعليمي فقط — وليس نصيحة استثمارية أو ضريبية أو قانونية.

Combined footer for graphics and templates:

Educational content only — not investment, tax, or legal advice. | محتوى تعليمي فقط — وليس نصيحة استثمارية أو ضريبية أو قانونية.

3.2 Long-form version — lead magnets, PDFs, workbooks, workshop decks (final page/slide)

EN:

This material is provided for general educational purposes only. It does not constitute investment, tax, legal, accounting, or insurance advice, and it is not tailored to any person’s individual circumstances. Before making financial decisions, consult a licensed professional in the relevant field. All example figures are illustrative only.

AR:

هذه المادة مُعدّة لأغراض تعليمية عامة فقط، ولا تُعدّ نصيحة استثمارية أو ضريبية أو قانونية أو محاسبية أو تأمينية، وهي غير مصمّمة لظروف أي شخص بعينه. قبل اتخاذ أي قرار مالي، يُرجى استشارة مختص مرخّص في المجال المعني. جميع الأرقام الواردة هي أمثلة توضيحية فقط.

3.3 Illustrative-data label

Any invented numbers — sample budgets, fictional shop examples, filled-in templates — carry the label مثال توضيحي / Illustrative example directly on the visual (as specified in LOGO_AND_VISUAL_IDENTITY_GUIDE.md). Invented examples are a teaching tool; unlabeled invented examples are misinformation.

3.4 Clarity chats and future paid work

Free “money clarity chats” (from Week 10 onward) open with one spoken sentence: “Quick note before we start — this is an educational conversation about organizing money, not investment, tax, or legal advice; for those I’ll happily point you to licensed professionals.” When pilot engagements begin (Month 12+), the same scope statement goes in writing in the engagement note (see FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §8).

4. Content Red Lines

Beyond scope, four standing rules for everything you publish:

  1. Practical, never political. Lebanon’s financial realities — multi-currency life, the cash economy, banking constraints — are addressed as practical problems with practical tools. No commentary on politics, policy, or the conduct of banks or officials. Not because you have no opinions, but because the brand is a calm, useful room — and that door keeps the room calm. If a hot financial news event breaks: you may explain terms people are suddenly hearing (“what does ‘haircut’ mean?”), never assign blame or predict outcomes.
  2. No fear-selling. You will never use panic (“your savings are evaporating — act NOW”) to drive engagement. The category is anxious enough; your differentiation is composure. Urgency about getting organized is fine; dread is not.
  3. No fabricated anything. No invented testimonials, no invented statistics, no invented client stories, no bought followers or engagement pods. If you cite a number, know its source; if you can’t source it, cut it or clearly present it as illustrative (§3.3).
  4. Disclose any paid or gifted promotion. If a company ever pays you, gifts you a subscription, or sponsors a post, say so plainly in the post (“Sponsored” / “بالتعاون مع”). Never endorse a financial product or platform you haven’t examined — and be slowest of all with anything promising returns. One paid post for a dubious platform can erase two years of trust.

5. The Referral-Triggers Table — When and How to Refer Out

This is the table every other document points to. The pattern is always the same: appreciate the question → state your boundary in one clause → refer → offer what you can do. Referring out is not a weakness; done warmly, it is the single strongest trust-builder you have.

When someone asks… You say (EN) You say (AR)
“Should I buy dollars / gold / crypto / this stock?” “That’s an investment decision, and I don’t give investment advice — a licensed financial advisor is the right person. What I can help with is making sure you can see clearly how much you could set aside each month.” «هذا قرار استثماري، وأنا لا أقدّم نصائح استثمارية — المستشار المالي المرخّص هو الشخص المناسب. ما أستطيع مساعدتك فيه هو أن ترى بوضوح كم يمكنك ادّخاره شهريًا.»

Practical notes: - In public comments, keep the referral to one short sentence and offer to continue in DM — never discuss anyone’s personal finances in a public thread, even if they volunteer details. If someone posts their real numbers in a comment, reply kindly without engaging the specifics and take it to DM. - Build a referral bench early. From Month 3–4 onward, cultivate relationships with 1–2 licensed accountants, a lawyer, and (when possible) a licensed financial advisor (OUTREACH_AND_NETWORKING_PLAYBOOK.md’s collaborator track). “I know exactly who to ask” is a far stronger sentence than “ask someone.” Bench members become collaborators — and later, the firm’s professional bench per FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §5. - Refer to a category of professional, not a guaranteed outcome. Recommend warmly, but never promise what the professional will conclude, and accept no referral fees while positioned as a neutral educator — if that ever changes at Rung 4, it is disclosed.

6. Confidentiality & Data Care

Twelve years in a law firm gave you professional-grade confidentiality reflexes. Keep them as brand policy:

People’s stories. - Anything anyone shares about their finances — in a DM, a clarity chat, a workshop hallway — is confidential by default, forever. No retelling “anonymized” stories casually in posts without the consent process below. - Never screenshot or quote a private conversation in content, even praise, without explicit permission.

Employers, past and present. - No internal details from Sami Nahas Law Firm — ever. Client matters from a law firm are confidential without expiry. Your stories from those years are about your systems, habits, and generalized lessons, never about cases, clients, or colleagues. - Same for TiKiT: no internal numbers, client names, campaign details, or workplace anecdotes identifying anyone. Additionally, per FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §8: no side-work on company time or resources, no overlapping clients, and honor your employment terms — when in doubt, disclose.

Data handling (grows with the ladder). - Now: keep any collected contact info (template requesters, kit downloads) in one private place, used only for what people asked for. No sharing, selling, or adding people to lists they didn’t request. - Rung 3–4: when real client records enter your life — statements, invoices, books — store them in a dedicated, access-controlled location (separate drive/folder, strong passwords, no shared family devices), share files only through private channels, and delete or return client data when an engagement ends. The firm’s client-data practice (FUTURE_CONSULTING_COMPANY_BLUEPRINT.md §7) starts as this personal habit.

7. Case Studies & Testimonials — The Consent Rules

Portfolio Project 3 (the bookkeeping-cleanup case study) and every future client story follow these rules exactly:

  1. Written consent first. A short message the person agrees to in writing (a saved chat message is fine at pilot stage): what will be shared, where, in which languages, and that they may withdraw before publication. Verbal agreement is not consent for publication.
  2. True anonymization — removing the name is not enough. Also remove or generalize: business type if identifying in their town, neighborhood, distinctive numbers, dates that pinpoint them, and any detail that lets an acquaintance guess. Test: would their neighbor recognize them? If maybe — generalize further or don’t publish.
  3. Change nothing material. Anonymize identity; never embellish results. “Fictionalized composite” stories, if ever used for teaching, are labeled as illustrative (§3.3), not presented as client outcomes.
  4. Testimonials are verbatim and consented — the person’s own words, with written permission, attributed the way they choose (full name, first name, or “salon owner, Beirut”). Never edited into stronger praise, never invented, never traded for discounts.
  5. Numbers in results are conservative and honest. “We found roughly $180/month of untracked spending” only if the sheet actually showed it.

8. When Things Go Wrong — Protocols

You published an error (wrong number, wrong definition). Correct it in the same place, visibly and fast: “Correction: I wrote X; the accurate version is Y — thank you [Name] for flagging it.” Don’t silently delete (edit-plus-note beats deletion; delete only if the error could cause real harm, and post the correction anyway). A graceful public correction builds more trust than an unblemished feed — this is the protocol from PERSONAL_BRAND_STRATEGY.md §9, and it works.

You realize you crossed a line (e.g., a DM reply drifted into “you should…” territory). Go back within 24 hours: “Rereading my message — I was more prescriptive than I should be. That decision is yours, ideally with a licensed advisor; here’s the educational picture instead.” Self-correction before anyone complains is integrity made visible.

Someone acts on your content and blames you for a bad outcome. Respond privately, calmly, without defensiveness and without accepting a fault that isn’t yours: express genuine sympathy, restate that the content was educational and general (your disclaimer discipline matters here), and where appropriate help them find the right professional. Never argue in comments. If anything ever escalates toward legal language — stop engaging and consult a lawyer before replying further.

Harassment, spam, or bad-faith trolling. One polite, factual reply maximum if a correction is warranted for onlookers; then disengage, and block where needed. You owe the public accuracy; you owe trolls nothing. Never delete honest critical comments — answer them well; they’re where authority is displayed.

Someone in genuine crisis contacts you (panic over debts, despair about their situation). Respond with humanity first, boundaries intact: acknowledge, be kind, and guide them toward proper support — a licensed professional for the finances and, if their distress sounds deeper than money, gently encourage them to talk to someone qualified to help with that too. You can be a caring human without becoming a counselor you’re not.

9. The Pre-Publish Checklist — 60 Seconds, Every Post

Before anything goes live, five questions:

  • Scope: educational, not personal prescription? (Section 1 test)
  • Claims: every credential/title mentioned actually held today, worded per Section 2?
  • Disclaimer: standard line present (both languages where the post is bilingual)?
  • Numbers & stories: real and sourced, or labeled illustrative? No confidential spillover, no identifiable person without consent?
  • Tone: calm, practical, non-political, no fear-selling?

Five green boxes → publish with a clear conscience. Any doubt on any box → hold the post and fix it; there is always another Monday.

10. Why This Document Is Your Best Marketing

One last reframe, because it’s the truth: nothing in these rules costs you growth. The advisors who overclaim are locked in an arms race of promises they can’t keep. You are running a different race — the one where, after months of watching you say “that’s not my lane, here’s who to ask, and here’s what I can genuinely help with,” people conclude the only thing that matters: “When she says something, it’s true.”

That sentence is the entire brand. This document is how you earn it — and keep it.
