Future Consulting Company Blueprint

For: Dorina Salman Status: LONG-TERM OPTION — not the immediate plan.

⚑ READ THIS FRAMING FIRST

This document describes a company that does not exist yet and should not exist yet.

It is a 24–36 month option, activated only when every item on the Launch Criteria checklist (Section 9) is met. The immediate plan — the next 12 months — is personal authority, skills, and career positioning, exactly as laid out in the master plan and 90-day action plan.

Why write the blueprint now, then? Three reasons: 1. Direction: knowing the destination makes every content post, certificate, and template a brick in a wall rather than scattered effort. 2. Confidence: you can speak about your trajectory with quiet certainty, because you have one. 3. Discipline: having the launch criteria written down protects you from launching too early — the most common way consulting dreams die.

Keep your job. Build the brand. Let demand arrive first.

1. Vision & Mission of the Future Firm

Vision: To be the most trusted name for financial clarity among individuals, families, freelancers, and small businesses in Lebanon and the Arabic-speaking world — the firm people call when money feels like fog.

Mission: We organize money. We turn shoeboxes of receipts into clean books, anxiety into cash-flow visibility, and confusion into calm, informed decisions — through hands-on financial operations support and plain-language education in Arabic and English.

What the firm is: a financial-clarity and financial-operations practice — bookkeeping support, cash-flow systems, budgeting frameworks, financial organization, and training.

What the firm is not, and will never claim to be: an investment advisory, a tax practice, a law firm, or an audit firm. Anything regulated is delivered with or by licensed partners (Section 7), never improvised.

2. Name Concept

Primary: Wudooh Finance — وضوح

  • Meaning: Wudooh (وضوح) is Arabic for clarity. It is short, elegant, easy to pronounce in both languages, and it is the brand promise. The bilingual identity is built in: “Wudooh Finance” reads as premium-international in English while carrying full meaning in Arabic.
  • Rationale: every strong service brand answers “what do I get?” in one word. Here, the answer is the name itself. It also links seamlessly to the taglines already in use: “Money, made clear.” / “المال بوضوح.”
  • Verbal identity: the personal brand (“Dorina Salman — finance-clarity specialist”) flows naturally into “founder of Wudooh Finance” when the day comes. Nothing has to be renamed or unwound.

Alternates (keep in reserve)

  1. Clear Ledger Consulting — concrete, professional, instantly signals bookkeeping and order. Weaker in Arabic (requires translation), stronger for an English-first market.
  2. Salman Financial Clarity — a family-name firm signals accountability and permanence; works well if the practice stays boutique and founder-led. Slightly less scalable if you later add partners.

⚠ Before any launch

Check trademark availability and commercial-name registration in Lebanon (and domain + social handle availability) for whichever name you choose. Do this with a local lawyer or through the Ministry of Economy and Trade / commercial registry channels. Names in this document are working concepts, not cleared marks.

3. Target Clients

Segment Who they are Their pain Why they choose Wudooh
Small businesses (roughly 1–20 staff: shops, agencies, clinics, restaurants, trading businesses) Owner-operators doing finances “in their head” or in a drawer No visibility on cash flow, messy or nonexistent books, surprises at month-end, multi-currency confusion Hands-on ops help in their own language, without big-firm cost or judgment
Freelancers & solo professionals (designers, developers, translators, consultants) Skilled earners with zero financial structure Mixed personal/business money, unprofessional invoicing, no records, feast-and-famine cash A one-time setup that makes them look and operate professionally
Families & individuals Households navigating real economic pressure No budget, no savings system, money stress and conflict Calm, judgment-free, culturally fluent guidance to organize the household’s money

Beachhead: start with freelancers and micro-businesses (fastest sales cycle, lowest complexity), expand to small businesses (highest revenue per client), keep family work as the mission-driven and referral-generating layer.

4. Service Packages — Five Fully Specced Offers

Pricing philosophy: the ranges below are indicative value anchors in USD, included so the packages are designed with economics in mind. They are not market research. Lebanon’s pricing environment (currency dynamics, purchasing power, cash norms) requires local calibration at launch time — test with pilot clients, adjust openly. Never quote these ranges as established market rates.

Package 1 — The Money Clarity Audit (entry point / flagship diagnostic)

  • Who it’s for: any client segment; the universal front door.
  • What’s included: a structured intake questionnaire; review of 3 months of financial records (statements, invoices, receipts, whatever exists); a 90-minute working session; application of the signature Money Clarity Audit framework.
  • Deliverables: a Clarity Report (5–8 pages): where the money actually goes, top 3 leaks or risks, a prioritized 30-day fix list, and a recommended next step.
  • Duration: 1–2 weeks elapsed; ~6–8 hours of work.
  • Indicative pricing: $150–$400 depending on complexity (individual vs. business).
  • Role in the model: low-risk first purchase that converts naturally into Packages 2–4.

Package 2 — Monthly Books & Cash-Flow Ops (recurring revenue core)

  • Who it’s for: small businesses and established freelancers.
  • What’s included: monthly transaction categorization and reconciliation (QuickBooks or Xero), a maintained 13-week cash-flow forecast, a monthly one-page owner’s dashboard (revenue, costs, cash position, receivables), and a 30-minute monthly review call.
  • Deliverables: clean monthly books, updated forecast, dashboard PDF, review-call summary with action items.
  • Duration: ongoing monthly retainer; 3-month minimum commitment.
  • Indicative pricing: $200–$600/month by transaction volume and complexity.
  • Role in the model: the backbone — predictable revenue, deep client relationships, referral engine.

Package 3 — Freelancer Finance Setup (productized one-time)

  • Who it’s for: freelancers and solo professionals.
  • What’s included: separation of personal and business money (accounts and habits), professional bilingual invoice system, income/expense tracking setup, records-keeping routine, basic pricing-and-buffer worksheet, one training session to hand it all over.
  • Deliverables: configured tracking workbook or app setup, invoice templates (AR/EN), records checklist, a personalized “your money routine” one-pager, session recording/notes.
  • Duration: 2 weeks; ~5–6 hours of work.
  • Indicative pricing: $120–$300 flat.
  • Role in the model: high-volume, highly shareable, feeds testimonials and content.

Package 4 — Family Budget Reset (mission-driven)

  • Who it’s for: households and couples who want order and calm around money.
  • What’s included: two 90-minute sessions (discovery + build), a customized household budget in the client’s preferred format, a savings-goal structure, a multi-currency handling approach where relevant, and a 30-day follow-up check-in.
  • Deliverables: the family’s working budget system, a one-page money routine for the household, follow-up adjustments.
  • Duration: ~5 weeks including follow-up.
  • Indicative pricing: $100–$250.
  • Boundaries: strictly organization and education — no investment, insurance-product, or debt-restructuring advice; referral triggers per RISK_ETHICS_AND_COMPLIANCE.md apply fully.
  • Role in the model: purpose, word-of-mouth, and the human stories (used only with consent and anonymization) that make the brand loved.

Package 5 — Workshops & Training (reach and B2B door-opener)

  • Who it’s for: companies (staff financial-wellness sessions), nonprofits and universities, co-working spaces, and online cohorts.
  • What’s included: 2–3 hour interactive workshops from a menu — Money Basics Made Simple, Cash-Flow for Small Business, Freelancer Finance 101 — delivered in Arabic, English, or bilingually; includes the relevant template pack for every attendee.
  • Deliverables: live session, slide deck, template pack, follow-up resource email.
  • Duration: half-day per delivery + half-day preparation once the deck library exists.
  • Indicative pricing: $250–$800 per corporate/institutional session; per-seat pricing for open cohorts.
  • Role in the model: authority at scale — one workshop seeds dozens of audit clients.

5. Delivery Model

Stage 1 — Solo practitioner (launch): you deliver everything; capacity ~6–10 active clients mixing retainers and one-time packages. Tools: QuickBooks/Xero, Google Workspace, a scheduling tool, and a simple CRM spreadsheet.

Stage 2 — Solo + licensed partners (months 6–18 post-launch): formal referral/subcontract relationships with a licensed accountant (tax filings, statutory requirements), a lawyer (contracts, registration questions), and optionally a licensed financial advisor for clients who need regulated investment guidance. You remain the client’s trusted front door; regulated work is done by regulated professionals, transparently.

Stage 3 — Small team (if demand justifies): a part-time bookkeeper handles Package 2 production; you focus on audits, training, relationships, and content. Only expand when retainer revenue reliably covers the added cost.

Rule at every stage: never let delivery quality dilute. Ten delighted clients build a firm; thirty adequately served ones quietly dismantle it.

The company inherits the personal-brand system already defined in LOGO_AND_VISUAL_IDENTITY_GUIDE.md — continuity is the point: clients follow you into the firm.

  • Palette: Deep Teal #0F4C5C (primary), Warm Gold #D4A937 (accent), Ivory #FAF7F0 (background), Charcoal #1F2937 (text).
  • Typography: Playfair Display (EN headings), Inter (EN body), Tajawal (Arabic).
  • Wordmark direction: “Wudooh Finance” set in Playfair Display with the Arabic وضوح as an integrated calligraphic mark (Concept B in the identity guide is purpose-built to graduate into this company mark). Bilingual lockup: Arabic and English given equal visual weight.
  • Voice: identical to the personal brand — warm, direct, practical, never condescending, never salesy-slick. The firm should feel like her, multiplied — not like a bank.

7. Legal & Setup Considerations in Lebanon

General guidance only — verify everything with a local lawyer and accountant before acting. Lebanese requirements and practice evolve, and this document is a strategy paper, not legal advice.

  1. Business registration: choose a structure with counsel (sole proprietorship / establishment vs. a limited-liability company such as an SARL). Weigh liability protection against cost and administrative burden; register the commercial name at the same time.
  2. Accounting & tax compliance: engage a licensed Lebanese accountant from day one for the firm’s own books, tax registration, and filings. A finance-clarity firm with messy compliance is a contradiction the market will not forgive.
  3. Professional insurance: ask a local broker about professional indemnity / errors-and-omissions cover for bookkeeping and advisory-adjacent services. Availability and terms vary — investigate before signing the first sizeable client.
  4. Regulated-services boundary: in Lebanon as elsewhere, investment advice, audit, tax representation, and legal services are regulated professions. The firm’s engagement letters must state its scope explicitly (financial organization, bookkeeping support, education) and its referral practice for everything else. Have the lawyer review the standard engagement letter template.
  5. Contracts & data: a standard bilingual services agreement (scope, fees, confidentiality, data handling, disclaimer of regulated advice) and a basic client-data protection practice (see the confidentiality section of RISK_ETHICS_AND_COMPLIANCE.md).
  6. Banking & invoicing: a dedicated business account and clean invoicing from the first client — the firm must model the hygiene it sells.

8. Pre-Launch Pilot Mode (Months 12–30, before incorporating)

Between “personal brand” and “company” sits the pilot phase — small paid engagements taken as an individual, part-time, while employed:

  • What to take: Money Clarity Audits, Freelancer Finance Setups, and small workshop gigs — bounded, deliverable-based work that fits evenings/weekends. Avoid open-ended retainers while employed full-time.
  • How to charge: simple written engagement note (scope, deliverable, fee, educational-services disclaimer), payment before or at delivery. Ask your accountant how to declare this side income correctly.
  • Employer integrity: no conflict with TiKiT — no overlapping clients, no company time or resources, and honor any obligations in your employment terms. If in doubt, disclose.
  • What pilots are for: proof of demand, pricing calibration, testimonials (with consent), and process refinement. Every pilot is a rehearsal for the firm.
  • Cap it: 1–2 active pilot engagements at a time. Pilots serve the launch decision; they must not burn you out before it.

9. LAUNCH CRITERIA — The Gate

Incorporate and go full-time only when ALL of the following are true. Review this checklist quarterly from Month 12 onward. If any box is unchecked, the answer is “not yet” — and that is a strategic answer, not a failure.

  • 12+ months of consistent authority content published (the 3-posts/week rhythm held, allowing for normal life interruptions).
  • Certificate portfolio complete: bookkeeping certificate, QuickBooks ProAdvisor, Xero Advisor at minimum (per FINANCE_SKILLS_CREDENTIALS_ROADMAP.md).
  • Demand signal: 5+ genuine inbound requests per month for paid help, sustained for 3 consecutive months (tracked in the measurement dashboard).
  • Proof of delivery: 2–3 paid pilot engagements completed part-time, with satisfied clients and at least 2 usable (consented) testimonials.
  • Financial runway: a minimum of 6 months of personal living expenses saved, separate from any business startup funds.
  • Professional bench in place: a named accountant and lawyer engaged; registration path and costs confirmed.
  • Gut check passed: you want this on a normal Tuesday, not just on inspired days.

10. What NOT to Do

  1. Do not quit your job early. Employment is your runway, your risk buffer, and — as a working administrator in a real company — part of your credibility. The job funds the dream until the dream funds itself.
  2. Do not incorporate before demand. A registered company with no clients is cost, paperwork, and pressure. Demand first, structure second — always.
  3. Do not take regulated work “just this once.” One improvised tax filing or investment recommendation can undo three years of trust-building. The bright lines in RISK_ETHICS_AND_COMPLIANCE.md apply to the firm even more than to the person.
  4. Do not underprice into exhaustion. Calibrate for the local market, yes — but free and near-free work should be content and templates, not consulting hours. Pilots are discounted, not free.
  5. Do not build the firm in secret. The audience that watched you learn is the firm’s first client list. Keep building in public right up to — and through — launch day.
  6. Do not let the blueprint rush you. This document will still be here in 24 months. So will the opportunity — bigger, because you kept building.

A company is a promise with a structure around it. Build the promise first.
