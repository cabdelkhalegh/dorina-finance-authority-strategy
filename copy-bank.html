9. Usage Notes — Arabic vs. English vs. Both

Post in Arabic when: the topic is everyday personal finance for a broad local audience (Pillar 1), or regional money realities (Pillar 4). Arabic finance content of real quality is scarce — it is your fastest route to standing out, and it reaches the people who need it most.

Post in English when: the topic targets professionals, recruiters, or the international/diaspora business audience (career-positioning posts, Pillar 2 for agency-type businesses, certificate announcements). English keeps Persona 3 — employers and recruiters — reading.

Post bilingually (EN + divider + AR in one post) when: it’s a milestone post (launch story, certificate completion, template release) or evergreen content you’ll pin to Featured. Bilingual milestone posts are the brand statement: clarity for everyone.

Practical rhythm: within your 3 posts/week, aim for roughly one Arabic, one English, one bilingual — then adjust toward whichever language your growing audience engages with more. Whatever the split, both taglines always appear together: Money, made clear. | المال بوضوح.

Quality rule for Arabic: always polished Modern Standard Arabic — natural and warm, never machine-literal. If a sentence sounds like a translation, rewrite it from the idea, not from the English words. When quoting everyday phrases people actually say (e.g., «أين ذهب راتبي؟»), that spoken flavor inside quotation marks is welcome — it keeps MSA content human.
